Charlie's AI OS · guide for beginners

What we've built, and why

An AI OS is a folder of plain notes that teaches Claude who you are and how you work, so every chat starts already knowing you. This guide explains each part in plain English, lists the skills you'll actually use, and shows the checks we ran to prove it works.

Why it all matters: you kept having to ask me about skills and tools you'd never heard of, and I kept asking you to approve things. Everything below fixes one of those two problems: the OS now finds and ranks tools by itself, explains them, and only asks you when it really needs you.

Eight parts

1. The rulebook (AGENTS.md)

What it is

A short staff handbook Claude reads before every reply: who you are, how to talk to you, where everything lives.

Why

Nate's newest video (and Anthropic) showed newer AI needs less telling. We cut it by about 40% and proved nothing was lost.

How you use it

You don't. It works in the background. Changes go through me.

2. The Decision Desk

What it is

One page holding every question I need you to answer. Tap an option, press Send.

Why

I was asking you to say "go" on small things and asking the same question four times. Now small things just happen, and real choices wait in one place.

How you use it

Open it when I say "one new question on the Desk". I read your answers at the start of each session.

3. One health check (the audit skill)

What it is

A check-up that scores the OS and spots wrong, stale or clashing notes. Now one skill instead of two, plus a "context doctor" that shows what Claude reads every message.

Why

Nate: re-check your set-up every time a new model comes out. Clutter quietly makes answers worse.

How you use it

Nothing: it runs by itself every Friday at 8:59am and notifies your phone. After a model switch, say "audit".

4. ENATE (Nate's brain)

What it is

Everything Nate teaches about building an AI OS, boiled down from 69 of his videos into 43 ideas and 43 rules, each with his exact words and a timestamp.

Why

So we follow a proven method instead of guessing, and can ask "what would Nate's method say?" any time. "Nate" now means the real person and his new videos; ENATE is our copy of his know-how.

How you use it

Ask "what does ENATE say about…". New Nate videos get added with the brain-ingest skill, which now checks titles and chapters first and only reads the videos that fill a gap, to save your usage.

5. The Hands Brain

What it is

Every tool, skill and plugin from The Next New Thing's weekly shows (newest first) and the skills.sh leaderboard of most-installed skills: 115 so far, ranked in 15 groups, newest video first, then back to week 38. Each new tool is compared with its group's number one, and on the newest show we also note what the presenters think of each tool and whether we agree. Older tools get crossed out when something newer replaces them.

Why

Tools change every week. You shouldn't have to watch every video or ask me what each one is. It also links each tool to the ENATE idea it fits.

How you use it

Open the map, tap a dot. Ask "what's the best free tool for…". Weekly update: "update the Hands Brain".

6. Charlie's Brain (the map)

What it is

A picture of the whole OS: every note, skill and idea as a dot, with lines where they connect.

Why

Our stand-in for Obsidian until you're on the Mac. Seeing it makes gaps and clutter obvious.

How you use it

Open it on your phone, tap around, mark anything confusing in Review.

7. The Voice Gym

What it is

Short practice messages written the way AI usually sounds: a text to a mate, a YouTube reply, a quote follow-up. You rewrite each one how you'd really send it.

Why

You said finding "your voice" is hard. The differences between my version and yours show it: what you cut, the words you use, how you sign off. Those become your voice notes, so drafts start sounding like you.

How you use it

Open it, rewrite one or two, tap Save. Typos and slang are fine.

8. The council of elders (planned)

What it is

Each brain gets a voice: ENATE, The Next New Thing, you, and later Nick and Karpathy. Ask one question and see where they agree and where they don't.

Why

One expert can be wrong. Seeing several side by side, each quoting its own sources, makes your decision clearer.

How you use it

Not yet. First step is running now: The Next New Thing's voice from its newest show. Plan: research/council-plan.md.

Words you'll hear

Context
Everything the AI can see while it answers you. Less but better context gives better answers.
Rulebook / router
AGENTS.md: the short handbook read before every reply. It points to where things live.
Skill
A recipe card for one job, read only when that job comes up.
Plugin
A bundle you install that can add several skills, helpers and connections at once.
MCP
A plug that connects Claude to another tool or service (email, a design app, documentation).
Agent
A helper Claude sends off to do one job, often on a cheaper model, then report back.
Repo
A project folder kept on GitHub, with every change saved and undoable.
Brain
A folder of distilled knowledge (ideas, rules, quotes) an advisor reads. ENATE and the Hands Brain are brains.
Transcript
The words of a video as text. Free to fetch, a few at a time.
Audit
A health check that finds clutter, stale facts and clashes.
Backtest
Re-running real questions against what we built to prove it works, not just claiming it.
