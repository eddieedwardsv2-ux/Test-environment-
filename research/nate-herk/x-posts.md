- **[2026-10-02](https://x.com/nateherk/status/2106096961799831642)** · 46 likes · 5242 views — I sat down with @daveebbelaar to discuss how AI has changed the rules of building apps.  Dave's been working in Python for over a decade, and now builds through Claude Code and Codex.  A few things I took away:  → Start with something you’ll use yourself. Dave described a ladder: personal tools, internal team tools, client projects, and public software products. Each step brings more responsibility. You can start small and grow as you learn.  → Get something in your hands early. Dave has moved toward building sooner and giving feedback on a working version. It’s easier to explain what feels wrong when you can actually use the thing.  → Give your agent a way to prove it works. Define what good looks like, provide examples, and set up tests. “Keep improving this” becomes much more useful when the agent can measure whether it’s making progress.  → Your taste needs examples. For subjective work, like customer replies or video editing, collect examples of what you like and dislike. Dave explained how those human judgments can help train an AI reviewer to better match your standards.  → Learn the basics before other people depend on your app. Understand how your front end, back end, and database connect. Security deserves attention early because a leaked customer email can’t be fixed by shipping a better version tomorrow.  As someone who came into this without a software engineering background, I found that encouraging.  An experienced developer and a beginner can now use the same tools. Experience still helps you know what to ask, what to check, and when to slow down.  That gives me a much clearer direction for what to keep learning as I build.
